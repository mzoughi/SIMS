A Wave Changing Medium

Readout

A light wave of frequency `f` travels in a medium of index `n_1` and meets a second medium of index `n_2`. In a medium of index `n` light travels at `v = c/n`. At the boundary part of the wave is reflected back into the first medium and part is transmitted into the second.

Space and time. The top view is a snapshot of the wave against position: the distance between two crests is the wavelength `lambda`. The probes record the wave against time at one place: the time between two crests is the period `T = 1/f`. The amplitude `A` is the height of a crest, which is also the depth of a trough, measured from the middle line.

The frequency does not change. The boundary is shaken by the incident wave, once per period, and it shakes the second medium at the same rate: `f_2 = f_1`, and so the period is the same on both sides. Since `v = f lambda`, the wavelength changes in proportion to the speed:

`lambda_1 = v_1/f`,   `lambda_2 = v_2/f`,   `lambda_2/lambda_1 = v_2/v_1 = n_1/n_2`.

Going into a slower medium (`v_2 < v_1`, larger index) the wavelength gets smaller; going into a faster one, larger.

The phase. The transmitted wave starts in step with the incident wave: its phase does not change. The reflected wave flips (a crest comes back as a trough, a change of 180°) when the second medium is slower (`n_2 > n_1`), and does not flip when it is faster (`n_2 < n_1`). For light at normal incidence the amplitudes are

`r = (n_1 - n_2)/(n_1 + n_2)`,   `t = (2 n_1)/(n_1 + n_2)`,

where a negative `r` is the flip. The boundary view compares the three waves right at the boundary. In the first medium you can show the incident wave, the reflected wave, both separately, or their sum (what is really there). The animation runs in slow motion: real light oscillates about `10^15` times per second.
