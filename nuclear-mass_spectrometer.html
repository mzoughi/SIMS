The Mass Spectrometer

A mass spectrometer sorts atoms by mass. Atoms are ionized in the source, pass through a velocity selector (crossed electric and magnetic fields) that lets through only ions of one speed, v = E/B, and then enter a second magnetic field B₀. There each ion moves on a half circle of radius r = mv/(qB₀): heavier ions make bigger circles and land farther along the detector. Each isotope of an element lands at its own place.

Reading the spectrum

A mass spectrometer separates ions by their mass-to-charge ratio. In the first tab (a Bainbridge spectrometer), ions leave the source with a range of speeds and enter a velocity selector, where an electric field `E` and a perpendicular magnetic field `B` push them in opposite directions. The electric force `qE` does not depend on speed, the magnetic force `qvB` does; they balance only for

`v = E/B`,

so only ions with this speed go straight through the slit; faster ones are pushed one way, slower ones the other. In the deflecting field `B_0` the magnetic force `qvB_0` is the centripetal force, `qvB_0 = mv^2/r`, so each ion moves on a half circle of radius

`r = (mv)/(qB_0) = (mE)/(qB B_0)`,

and lands a distance `2r` from the slit. Since all ions have the same speed, `r` is proportional to `m/q`: isotopes, which differ only by their number of neutrons, land at different places, and the number landing at each place gives their abundance. A doubly charged ion makes a circle half as large.

In the second tab the ions are accelerated through a voltage `V`, so `qV = 1/2 mv^2`, and bent by a magnet on a fixed radius `r` (the flight tube): `m/q = (B^2 r^2)/(2V)`. Scanning `B` brings each `m/z` to the detector in turn, giving the mass spectrum: the ion current against `m/z`. Molecules are often broken into fragments by the ionizing electrons, so a spectrum shows the molecular ion, its isotopes and its fragments; the pattern identifies the substance. The resolution `m/(Delta m)` is set by the slit widths: a low resolution merges neighbouring peaks. Isotope masses and abundances are standard values; the fragment intensities are typical, approximate values; the animation is slowed enormously.
