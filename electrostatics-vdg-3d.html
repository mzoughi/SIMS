The Van de Graaff Generator

a belt carries charge to a metal dome · V = Q/C · the air breaks down near 3 MV/m

HOW IT WORKS
The generator
The demonstration

1. How it works. Most classroom generators rely on the triboelectric effect, charging by contact between two different materials, rather than on a high-voltage supply. A motor turns a rubber belt over a lower roller. When two materials touch and separate, the one lower on the triboelectric series keeps some electrons of the other. A silicone (or Teflon) roller is lower than rubber: it strips electrons from the belt, so the roller becomes negative and the belt leaves it positive. The grounded lower comb keeps the roller's charge from building up without limit.

The positive belt carries its charge up into the hollow metal dome, past a sharp metal comb connected to the dome. The field at the sharp tips is strong enough to ionize the air in the gap: electrons are pulled off the comb onto the belt, neutralizing it. Electrons leaving the comb leave the dome short of electrons, and in a conductor that net charge sits on the outer surface, where it does not oppose the next charge arriving inside. If the roller is higher on the series than the belt (glass, nylon or aluminium with a rubber belt), the belt takes electrons from the roller instead, carries them up, and the dome becomes negative. Two identical materials do not charge at all.

An isolated sphere of radius `R` has `C = 4 pi epsilon_0 R`, `V = Q/C` and `E_(surface) = V/R`. The potential rises until the surface field approaches the dielectric strength of dry air, `E_b ~~ 3 xx 10^6` V/m: the air at the surface ionizes and the dome loses charge into the air as a faint violet glow, the corona discharge (visible in a dark room). The potential stops near `V_(max) ~~ E_b R`. Humid air also lets charge leak away.

2. Hair raising. A person on an insulating stool touching the dome becomes part of it. Every hair carries charge of the same sign: the hairs repel and stand out along the field, then stay nearly still. Even without touching, a person near a charged dome is affected: a grounded person acquires an induced charge of the opposite sign, and an insulated person is polarized. The fine hairs on the arms rise, which you can feel. 3. Bubbles. A neutral bubble is polarized and pulled toward the dome. Many bubbles pick up charge from the ions of the corona before they touch, and the rest on contact; charged bubbles are repelled. 4. Pie plates lift off when the electric pressure `epsilon_0 E^2//2` on the top plate exceeds its weight. 5. A thread tied to the dome is attracted to a nearby grounded hand, touches it, discharges and returns. 6. Sparks jump to a grounded sphere when the gap reaches its breakdown voltage, approximately `E_b d//(1 + 6d)` (d in metres).

7. CFL bulb. The field of the dome makes a potential difference between the ends of a fluorescent bulb held near it, `Delta V ~~ V R (1/r_1 - 1/r_2)`. It drives a current through the mercury vapour, which glows, with no wires. Pointing at the dome gives the largest `Delta V`; held sideways, both ends are at nearly the same potential and it stays dark. 8. Electric whirl. The field at the sharp tips ionizes the air; ions of the same sign as the dome are repelled from the tips (an “ion wind”), and by Newton's third law the tips are pushed backward: the whirl turns. With blunt ends there is no corona and no rotation. 9. A sharp point. A grounded sharp point (a key, a wire) has a very strong field at its tip. The air there ionizes at a low potential, and ions of the opposite sign flow to the dome and neutralize it silently: the dome is drained, its potential stays far below the spark voltage, and a hand can then be brought close without a spark. A blunt knuckle gives sparks instead. This is the principle of the lightning rod.

Belt current, leakage, corona and point currents, body capacitance, masses and the breakdown formulas are typical, approximate values chosen for a classroom generator. The triboelectric order used: glass, nylon, wool, aluminium, paper, hard rubber, vinyl, silicone, Teflon; real series vary with the source.
