Beats — and How a Piano Tuner Uses Them

Two sound waves with slightly different frequencies go in and out of step. When they are in step they add (loud); half a beat later they cancel (soft). The loudness rises and falls |f₂ − f₁| times per second: these are beats. Listen to each tone alone, then both together. The slow beat can be hard to pick out: Beat rhythm adds a click at every loud moment, and Difference tone plays |f₂ − f₁| itself as a pitch (when it is high enough to hear). Start with the volume low.

How it works

Two sound waves of frequencies `f_1` and `f_2` and equal amplitude `A` add up, at a fixed place, to

`p = A cos(2 pi f_1 t) + A cos(2 pi f_2 t) = [2A cos(2 pi (f_2 - f_1)/2 t)] cos(2 pi (f_1 + f_2)/2 t)`.

We hear a tone at the average frequency `(f_1 + f_2)//2` whose loudness rises and falls. The bracket, the envelope, reaches its largest size twice per cycle of `cos(2 pi (f_2 - f_1)/2 t)`, so the loudness peaks `f_beat = |f_2 - f_1|` times per second: the beat frequency. If the amplitudes differ, the sound never falls to silence.

Hearing the beat. The sum contains only the frequencies `f_1` and `f_2`: there is no wave at `|f_2 - f_1|`. A few beats per second are a rhythm of loudness, not a pitch (below about 20 Hz the ear does not hear a tone at all). The “Beat rhythm” button adds a click at each loudness maximum to help students follow it. When the two tones are loud and farther apart, the ear itself (which does not respond perfectly linearly) can create a faint difference tone (Tartini tone) at `|f_2 - f_1|`; the “Difference tone” button plays that pitch directly so students know what to listen for. Between about 15 and 30 Hz the beats blur into roughness, and with larger differences two separate notes are heard.

Piano tuning. The frequency of a string fixed at both ends is `f = 1/(2L) sqrt(T/mu)`, so tightening the tuning pin (raising the tension `T`) raises the frequency. A tuner sounds a tuning fork and the matching string together and adjusts the pin until the beats slow down and vanish. The beat rate says how far off the string is, but not in which direction: if the beats get faster as you tighten, the string was already sharp. The forks are tuned to equal temperament with A4 = 440 Hz. Real pianos have two or three strings per note and slightly inharmonic overtones; these are left out. Start any sound with a low volume.
