Standing Waves in a Clarinet

Note:
Show: Harmonic:

A clarinet's air column is closed at the mouthpiece (by the reed) and open at the other end, so it behaves as a closed-open pipe: only odd harmonics fit, and the lowest note has a wavelength four times the column's length.

`f_n = n v/(4 L_"eff")`,   `n = 1, 3, 5, 7`.

Opening finger holes shortens the effective length and raises the note. The register key makes the clarinet jump to its third harmonic, a twelfth higher (an octave plus a fifth), not an octave as on a flute. The displacement or pressure of the standing wave and the motion of the air molecules can be shown, slowed down if needed.
