The image shows a laser beam entering a glass block shaped like a trapezoid. Inside the block the beam is bent (refracted) or reflected at each surface it meets. This tool lets you trace and measure its path.

1. The scale. Press “Set the scale”, click two marks on the ruler in the image, and type the distance between them: the tool computes how many millimetres one pixel of the image is. Lengths can then be measured with “Measure a length”.

2. The normal. Angles in optics are measured from the normal, the line perpendicular to the surface at the point where the beam meets it. Press “Draw a normal”, click two points along the edge of the block (a line parallel to the surface appears), then click the point where the beam meets the surface. The normal is drawn through that point, on both sides of the surface, and the helper line disappears. The normals are numbered N1, N2, …

3. The angle. Press “Measure an angle” and click three points: a point on the beam, the point of contact (the vertex), and a third point. The angle between the two lines is drawn, numbered θ1, θ2, … and entered in the table, where you can add a note (for example “incidence, first surface”). The earlier measurements stay on the image. With “snap” on, a click near a point already drawn (such as the foot of a normal) jumps to it.

Snell’s law relates the angles on the two sides of a surface: `n_1 sin theta_1 = n_2 sin theta_2`. When the beam goes from glass to air at more than the critical angle, `sin theta_c = n_"air"/n_"glass"`, it cannot leave: it is totally reflected.

Keyboard. Focus the image and use the arrow keys to move the cross (Shift: 10 pixels at a time), Enter to click there. The magnifier and the zoom help place each click precisely. Your work is kept in this browser.
