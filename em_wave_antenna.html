An antenna, driven by an oscillating current `I_0`, sends out an electromagnetic wave of wavelength `lambda`. The electric field `vec E` oscillates along the antenna's direction and the magnetic field `vec B` perpendicular to it; both are perpendicular to the direction the wave travels, and they oscillate in step. Turning the antenna (angle `theta`) turns the plane in which `vec E` oscillates: the wave's polarization.

`c = lambda f`,   `omega = 2 pi f`,   `k = 2 pi / lambda`,   `B_0 = E_0/c`.

The animation speed is illustrative, not real time, and the `vec B` arrows are drawn as long as the `vec E` arrows for clarity, though in SI units `B` is far smaller.
