The Mirascope: A Toy That Floats

two facing parabolic mirrors form a real image of the toy above the hole

THE ILLUSION
The mirrors
The toy

A mirascope (sold as the “Mirage”) is made of two identical concave parabolic mirrors facing each other. The top mirror has a hole in its centre. A small toy, such as a ladybug, a pig, a frog or a tiger cub, sits on the bottom mirror. Looking in from above, you see a solid-looking toy sitting in the hole. Reach for it and your fingers pass through: it is a real image, formed by light that really does converge there.

The mirrors. Each mirror is a paraboloid, `y = r^2/(4f)`, where `f` is its focal length. In the commercial toy the separation of the vertices equals the focal length, `c = f`, so each mirror's focus lies at the vertex of the other: the toy sits at the focus of the top mirror and the hole is at the focus of the bottom mirror. The two mirrors meet at the rim, of radius `R = sqrt(2fc)`.

How the image forms. Light from the toy goes up to the top mirror. Since the toy is at that mirror's focus, the reflected light travels down parallel to the axis. The bottom mirror focuses the parallel light at its own focus, which is the hole. Each mirror obeys

`1/d_o + 1/d_i = 1/f`,   `m = -d_i/d_o`.

Applying it twice, a point at height `y` above the bottom vertex has its image at height `y_i ~~ f^2/(f-y)`, close to `f + y` for a small toy, with lateral magnification `m = m_1 m_2 ~~ -1`. The image is the same size and upright, but every point is carried to the opposite side of the axis: the image is the toy turned 180° about the vertical axis. Two reflections turn the image without mirror-reversing it. The yellow tag on each toy's right side stays on the image's right side.

Seeing it. The light that forms the image comes up through the hole from the bottom mirror. You see the image only if the line from your eye through the image, continued backward, passes through the hole and reaches the bottom mirror. Look in from too low an angle and the image vanishes. The toy itself is hidden under the top mirror, except when you look almost straight down through the hole.

Changing the mirrors. When `c` is not equal to `f`, the image moves up or down, changes size, and may stop being a real image above the hole. Moving the toy off-centre moves the image to the opposite side.

In the Inside tab the near part of the mirrors is cut away. Two rays leave the top of the toy, reflect at G on the top mirror and at E and I on the bottom mirror, and cross at J, the image point. The rays are traced exactly on the paraboloids; the image of the whole toy is computed from the mirror equation (the paraxial approximation), so the traced point J and the drawn image differ a little for points far from the axis. The scene is drawn with flat shaded polygons; the reflections on the mirror surfaces themselves are not drawn.
