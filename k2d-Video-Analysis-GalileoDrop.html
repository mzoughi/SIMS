This tool turns a video into motion data, the way video analysis is done in the lab. Here everyone analyses the same video, provided with the question, so there is nothing to upload: a ball dropped from a moving truck is tracked, frame by frame.

First a known length in the video sets the scale, in metres per pixel. Then an origin and the direction of the x axis are set; the axis can be tilted to follow a ramp or a track. Each click on the object then gives its position `(x, y)` at the time of that frame; the clock starts at the first marked point.

The velocity at each point is found from that point and the one before it:

`v_x(t_i) ~~ (x_i - x_(i-1))/(t_i - t_(i-1))`,   `v_y(t_i) ~~ (y_i - y_(i-1))/(t_i - t_(i-1))`.

Any two quantities can be graphed against each other. A linear fit gives the slope and its uncertainty: the slope of `v_y` against `t` is the acceleration, for example `-g` for a ball in free fall. The average and standard deviation of the plotted values can also be shown; a constant `v_x` is the sign of no horizontal force. The data are saved in this browser, so they survive a reload.

This tool is useful for studying kinematics, projectile motion, velocity and acceleration, and for practising measurement and uncertainty.
