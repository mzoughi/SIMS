This simulation shows a micrometer (screw gauge), an instrument for measuring small lengths such as the diameter of a wire or the thickness of a plate to a hundredth of a millimeter. The object is held between the fixed anvil and the spindle, which moves when the thimble is turned.

The fixed barrel carries a linear scale: marks above the center line show whole millimeters and marks below it show the half millimeters. The rotating thimble carries a circular scale of 50 divisions. One full turn of the thimble moves the spindle by one half millimeter, so each division of the thimble corresponds to

`0.5" mm"/50 = 0.01" mm"`.

The reading is the last mark uncovered on the barrel (whole and half millimeters) plus the thimble division that lines up with the center line, times 0.01 mm:

`"reading" = "barrel reading" + n xx 0.01" mm"`.

Drag the red dot on the thimble, or use the slider, to open and close the micrometer. Choose no object to practice reading the scales, or a disk or a rectangle to measure. The smallest division sets the precision of a single reading; in the best case the uncertainty is half of it, `+- 0.005" mm"`.

This simulation is useful for practicing the reading of a micrometer, understanding how a screw thread turns a small rotation into a precise linear distance, and estimating the uncertainty of a measurement.
