Readout

A truck moves at a constant velocity, and a person on it tosses a ball straight up, as seen from the truck. Seen from the ground, the ball also moves forward at the truck’s speed, since it had that horizontal velocity when it left the hand.

Without air, no horizontal force acts on the ball, so its horizontal velocity stays equal to the truck’s: it stays right above the thrower and comes back down into the hand. From the ground its path is a parabola; from the truck it goes straight up and down.

With air, the ball moves through still air, so it feels a drag force opposite to its velocity relative to the air: `vec F_D = -1/2 rho C_D A v vec v`, with `A` the area of its cross-section and `C_D approx 0.47` for a sphere. The horizontal part of the drag slows the ball down while the truck keeps going, so the ball falls behind and comes down behind the thrower. Seen from the truck, it is as if a wind blew backward.

How much drag matters depends on the ball: the drag grows with its cross-section (`d^2`) while its mass grows with its volume (`d^3`), so a larger ball of the same material is affected less. A light ball (like a ping-pong ball) drifts back much more than a heavy one.
