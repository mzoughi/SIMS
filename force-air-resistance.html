Air density 1.20 kg/m³; drag coefficient of a sphere Cd = 0.47; g = 9.81 m/s². Changing the ball or the height brings it back to the top.

A ball of mass `m` and radius `r` is dropped from rest from a large height. Two forces act on it: its weight `W = m g`, downward and constant, and the drag of the air, upward, which grows with the speed:

`D = 1/2 rho C_d A v^2`,   `A = pi r^2`.

At the start `v = 0`, so `D = 0` and the ball falls with acceleration `g`. As it speeds up, the drag grows and the net force `W - D` gets smaller, so the ball speeds up less and less. Its speed approaches the terminal velocity, where drag equals weight and the net force is zero:

`v_t = sqrt((2 m g)/(rho C_d A))`.

A heavier ball of the same size has a larger terminal velocity (it needs more drag to balance its weight); a larger ball of the same mass has a smaller one. The speed reaches about 76% of `v_t` after a time `v_t/g`, and about 96% after `2 v_t/g`.
