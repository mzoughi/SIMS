The Doppler Effect

Source
V = 0.5 vw
xi = 0.10 W
Observer
V = -0.3 vw
xi = 0.95 W

A sound source and an observer move along a line while the source sends out wavefronts at a steady rate. The wavefronts bunch up ahead of a moving source and spread out behind it, and an observer moving toward the waves meets them more often. Both raise or lower the frequency the observer hears, the Doppler effect. With speeds counted positive when the source and the observer move toward each other,

`f' = f (v + v_o)/(v - v_s)`,

where `v` is the speed of sound, `v_s` the source's speed and `v_o` the observer's. Speeds are given as fractions of `v_w`, the wave speed. The two sound buttons let you hear the source's tone and the tone the observer receives.
