This simulation sends a continuous longitudinal wave along a slinky. A hand at the left end pushes and pulls the first coil back and forth at a frequency `f`, with a displacement amplitude `A`. Each coil then moves back and forth along the slinky, the same direction the wave travels, about its own resting place: the coils do not travel with the wave, only the pattern of compressions (coils bunched together) and rarefactions (coils spread apart) does.

The speed of the wave depends on how stiff the slinky is and how heavy it is. For a slinky of length `L`, spring constant `K` and mass `M`,

`v = L sqrt(K/M)`,   `lambda = v/f`.

The wavelength `lambda` is the distance between two neighbouring compressions (or rarefactions). A stiffer slinky carries the wave faster; a heavier one, slower. The amplitude does not change the speed. Here the slinky is `L = 6` m long.

The coils are tinted by how bunched they are: warm where they are compressed, cool where they are stretched. The tracked-coil markers, the wavelength guide, the compression and rarefaction markers and the horizontal caliper help measure the wave; the ⏱ button times it.
