Van de Graaff Generator

Radius (25–50 cm): 40 cm

This simulation shows a Van de Graaff generator. A motor turns a belt that picks up charge at the bottom and carries it up into a hollow metal sphere, where it moves to the outer surface. The radius of the sphere can be changed.

The sphere is a capacitor: an isolated sphere of radius `R` has capacitance

`C = 4 pi epsilon_0 R`,

about 1.11 pF for every centimetre of radius. As charge `Q` builds up, the potential of the sphere rises:

`V = Q/C`.

The field at the surface, `E = V/R`, also grows. When it reaches the dielectric strength of air (about `3 xx 10^6` V/m), the air breaks down and sparks discharge the sphere, so no more charge can accumulate. Since `E = kQ/R^2` at the surface, the largest charge a sphere can hold grows as `R^2`: a larger sphere reaches a higher potential.

This simulation is useful for studying capacitance, electric potential, and dielectric breakdown.
