Radio waves cover roughly 3 kHz to 300 GHz; microwaves are simply the upper part of that range, about 300 MHz to 300 GHz. So cell-phone and Wi-Fi signals are both radio waves and microwaves. (A microwave oven uses 2.45 GHz, the same band as Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, but at a far higher power.) Values are approximate and vary by country.

Information cannot be sent efficiently at its own low frequencies: an antenna must be comparable to the wavelength, and `lambda = c//f` is 15 km at 20 kHz. A transmitter therefore sends a high-frequency carrier wave and modulates it: it changes the amplitude, the frequency or the phase of the carrier to carry the message.

AM (540–1700 kHz) changes the amplitude: `s(t) = A_c [1 + mu cos(2 pi f_m t)] cos(2 pi f_c t)`. Using `cos a cos b = 1/2 [cos(a - b) + cos(a + b)]`,

`s(t) = A_c cos(2 pi f_c t) + (mu A_c)/2 cos(2 pi (f_c - f_m) t) + (mu A_c)/2 cos(2 pi (f_c + f_m) t)`:

the AM wave is exactly the sum of three waves, the carrier and two side frequencies; they beat, and the beats are the envelope. FM (88–108 MHz) keeps the amplitude constant and changes the frequency: `s(t) = A_c cos(2 pi f_c t + beta sin(2 pi f_m t))`, `beta = Delta f//f_m`, with side frequencies `f_c +- n f_m` of amplitudes `J_n(beta)`.

Digital signals send bits. The simplest schemes switch the carrier on and off (ASK), between two frequencies (FSK, used in key fobs and, in a smoother form, Bluetooth) or between two phases (BPSK, used by GPS). Phones and Wi-Fi use quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM): each symbol sets both the amplitude and the phase,

`s(t) = I cos(2 pi f_c t) - Q sin(2 pi f_c t) = sqrt(I^2 + Q^2) cos(2 pi f_c t + phi)`, `tan phi = Q//I`,

again a sum of two waves of the same frequency, 90° apart. With `M` allowed points `(I, Q)` (the constellation), each symbol carries `log_2 M` bits: 2 for QPSK, 4 for 16-QAM, 6 for 64-QAM, 8 for 256-QAM. Denser constellations carry more bits but are more easily confused by noise, so a phone uses dense ones close to the tower and simpler ones far away. 4G, 5G and Wi-Fi send QAM on hundreds or thousands of closely spaced subcarriers at once (OFDM), so the transmitted signal is itself a sum of very many waves. For a 20 MHz 4G channel this gives about `1200 xx 14000 ~~ 1.7 xx 10^7` symbols per second before overhead.

The graphs are not to scale in time: a real carrier makes hundreds to many thousands of cycles for each sound cycle, bit or symbol. The drawing slider sets how many are drawn; the readouts give the real values. Frequencies and data rates are typical, approximate values.
