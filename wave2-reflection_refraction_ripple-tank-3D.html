Plane water waves meet a boundary between two depths of water, where they travel at different speeds. The water surface is shown in 3D, with the heights exaggerated; the sides of the tank show the floor, with a step at the boundary. On water, waves travel faster where it is deeper (for shallow water, `v = sqrt(g d)`), so a step in the floor is a boundary between two media. Turn and tilt the view by dragging on the water, or use the 3D, Top and Side buttons next to the play controls (play/pause, step back, step forward and restart).

Part of the wave is reflected, at the same angle as it arrived, and part is transmitted, bending toward or away from the normal. The frequency is the same on both sides, so the wavelength changes with the speed:

`(sin theta_i)/v_1 = (sin theta_t)/v_2`,   `lambda = v/f`,

`R = (v_2 - v_1)/(v_1 + v_2)`,   `T = (2 v_2)/(v_1 + v_2)`,

where `R` and `T` are the reflected and transmitted amplitudes as fractions of the incident one. In the 3D view the crests crowd closer together (shorter wavelength) in the slower, shallower water. The ray diagram is drawn just above the water; drag the boundary and the white square marker.
