An atomic nucleus is made of protons and neutrons, together called nucleons. Choose a nucleus by its atomic number `Z` (the number of protons, which decides the element) and its mass number `A` (the number of nucleons). The number of neutrons is `N = A - Z`, and the nucleus is written `\ _Z^A X`.

Size. Nucleons are packed like marbles, so the volume of a nucleus is proportional to `A`: `V = 4/3 pi r^3 = kA`, giving `r = r_0 A^(1/3)` with `r_0 ~~ 1.2` fm. The density, about `2.3 xx 10^17` kg/m³, is nearly the same for all nuclei.

Mass. Masses are given in atomic mass units, 1 u = `1.66054 xx 10^(-27)` kg (1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom). A nucleus is always lighter than its separate protons and neutrons; the difference, the mass defect `Delta m`, corresponds to the binding energy `E = Delta m c^2`. Atomic masses include the electrons, so the comparison uses hydrogen-1 atoms for the protons. The atomic mass of an element is the average of the masses of its natural isotopes weighted by their abundances, for example `m_(Cu) = (62.9296)(0.6909) + (64.9278)(0.3091) = 63.55` u.

Stability. On the chart of the nuclides each tile is one nucleus (rows: same `Z`, the isotopes of an element; columns: same `N`). Stable nuclei form a narrow band. Light stable nuclei have `N ~~ Z`; heavier ones need more neutrons than protons, because every proton repels every other proton through the long-range Coulomb force, while the strong nuclear force attracts only neighbouring nucleons. Masses, abundances and half-lives are tabulated values for the isotopes listed in the SIM (rounded); other combinations are estimated with the liquid-drop model and marked as estimates.
