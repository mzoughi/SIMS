Up to five point sources make circular waves on the water of a ripple tank, each with its own frequency, amplitude, phase and position. The water surface is shown in 3D: crests rise and troughs dip (the heights are exaggerated so they are easy to see). Turn and tilt the view by dragging on the water, or use the 3D, Top and Side buttons next to the play controls. The top view looks like a photograph of a real ripple tank.

It starts with two identical sources at the edge of the tank, 320 mm apart. “Identical sources” makes every source share the frequency, amplitude and phase of the one you edit (their positions stay independent). “Equally spaced row” keeps the sources in a row with the chosen separation; dragging any source then moves the whole row.

Where waves from two sources overlap they interfere: crest on crest builds a bigger wave, crest on trough cancels it. For two sources with the same frequency, at a point whose distances to them differ by `Delta d`, and with a phase difference `Delta phi` between the sources,

constructive: `(2 pi Delta d)/lambda - Delta phi = 2 pi m`,   destructive: `(2 pi Delta d)/lambda - Delta phi = (2m + 1) pi`,   `lambda = v/f`.

With the sources in phase (`Delta phi = 0`) this is `Delta d = m lambda` and `Delta d = (m + 1/2) lambda`. Changing the phase of one source shifts the whole pattern sideways. In the 3D view, the lines of destructive interference (nodal lines) are where the water stays flat. The buoy records the water level at one point over time, and the intensity along the dashed line shows the bright and dark bands, the same pattern as light through a double slit.
