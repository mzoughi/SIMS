This simulation shows how a concave (converging) mirror forms an image. The object stands in front of the mirror, on the left; the focal point F is on the axis at the distance `f` in front of the mirror, and the centre of curvature C at `R = 2f`. Three principal rays leave the top of the object: one parallel to the axis, which is reflected through F; one through F, which is reflected parallel to the axis; and one to the centre of the mirror (the vertex), which is reflected symmetrically about the axis. Where the reflected rays meet is the image of the top of the object.

For a spherical mirror of focal length `f = R/2`, an object at the distance `p_o` in front of the mirror forms an image at the distance `p_i` given by the mirror equation

`1/p_o + 1/p_i = 1/f`,

with the magnification

`m = h_i/h_o = -p_i/p_o`.

A positive `p_i` is a real image in front of the mirror, where the reflected light really meets: it can be caught on a screen. A negative `p_i` is a virtual image behind the mirror: the reflected rays only seem to come from it (dashed), so it can only be seen by looking into the mirror. A negative `m` means the image is inverted. Beyond C the image is real, inverted and reduced; between C and F it is real, inverted and enlarged; at F there is no image (the reflected rays are parallel); closer than F it is virtual, upright and enlarged, like a shaving or make-up mirror.

Three small views show the object as it is, what the eye sees from its position, and what appears on the screen. The object can be shown as the letter F or as a clown; neither looks the same when it is turned around, so the views show clearly whether the image is upright or upside down. When the screen or the eye is not at the right place, the picture is blurred. The screen and the eye are placed in front of the mirror, at the distance shown on their sliders. With "More rays", many more rays from the top of the object are drawn. The measuring lines help read positions (from the mirror, positive in front) and heights on the diagram.
