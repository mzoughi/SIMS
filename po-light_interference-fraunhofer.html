The scales compared

In the ripple tank the sources, the wavelength and the distance to the interference pattern are all about the same size, so the whole pattern fits in one picture. For light they are not: the slits are a fraction of a millimetre apart, the wavelength is about a thousand times smaller still, and the screen is about a metre away. No single picture can show all of it, so this SIM shows the same light pattern in three panels, each with its own scale (given in its title).

1. Near the slits (micrometres across). Light leaves every slit toward the point P on the screen. Because P is so far away, the rays to it are practically parallel. A line drawn perpendicular to them (dashed) is a line of equal distance to P, so the extra distance one ray travels is the highlighted segment, the path difference. The true angle is tiny, so by default it is drawn larger (the factor is shown); untick “Exaggerate” to see the true angle.

`Delta = d sin theta`

2. In between (not to scale). The distance `L` is thousands of times larger than `d`, so it is broken. The rays from different slits to P meet at an angle of only a few arc-seconds.

3. The screen (millimetres across). Bright fringes where the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths, dark ones in between:

bright: `d sin theta = m lambda`,   `tan theta = x/L`,   spacing `Delta x ~~ (lambda L)/d`,

`I/I_0 = ((sin(N phi//2))/(N sin(phi//2)))^2 ((sin beta)/beta)^2`,   `phi = (2 pi d sin theta)/lambda`,   `beta = (pi a sin theta)/lambda`.

The second factor (the single-slit envelope) is included only when the slits have a finite width `a`. More slits (`N > 2`, a grating) make the bright fringes narrower. This is the Fraunhofer (far-field) model: it replaces the exact path difference `r_1 - r_2` by `d sin theta`, which is excellent when `L` is much larger than `D^2/lambda`, `D` being the width of the slit array. The readout compares the two; for light the error is usually a tiny fraction of a wavelength. The usual rule of thumb is `L >> D^2/lambda`; it is cautious, and the pattern is often already very close to the far-field one when `L` is a little smaller (watch the error in the readout). (The ripple-tank SIM, by contrast, uses the exact distances, which is why its fringes curve near the sources.)
