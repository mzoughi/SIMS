This simulation shows the pattern made by two slits, each of width `a`, a distance `d` apart, lit by one or several light sources at the same time. For each wavelength, two effects combine. The light from the two slits interferes: bright fringes appear where the paths differ by a whole number of wavelengths,

`d sin theta = m lambda`,

so the fringes are evenly spaced, `Delta y ~~ (lambda L)/d` apart. And each slit diffracts the light: the single-slit pattern, with its dark fringes at `a sin theta = m lambda`, sets an envelope over the fringes,

`I = I_0 ((sin beta)/beta)^2 cos^2 phi`,   `beta = (pi a sin theta)/lambda`,  `phi = (pi d sin theta)/lambda`.

Light of different colours does not interfere with each other: each wavelength makes its own pattern, and the patterns simply add up on the screen. Longer wavelengths (red) give wider fringes than shorter ones (violet), so the colours separate away from the centre, while at the central maximum, where every colour is bright, the light mixes (red and green make yellow). With colour addition off, each source is drawn in its own lane, to compare the patterns side by side. The face-on view, the intensity curves (one per source) and the marker, which reads `I/I_0` for each source, help measure the fringe spacing of each colour.
