Shock Waves — Sonic Boom

Source Speed
Vsource = 1.20 vw

# = 5

When a source moves faster than the sound it makes, every new wavefront starts outside the earlier ones. The wavefronts pile up along a cone, the shock wave, heard on the ground as a sonic boom. The cone is narrower the faster the source:

`sin theta = v_w/v_s = 1/M`,

where `theta` is the half-angle of the cone and `M = v_s/v_w` the Mach number. The triangle drawn on one reference wavefront shows where the relation comes from: in the time the wavefront grows by `v_w t`, the source travels `v_s t`.
