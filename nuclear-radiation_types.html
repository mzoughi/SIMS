The radiation
Why it happens & where it moves on the chart

An unstable nucleus can change into a more stable one by emitting radiation. Each tab shows one kind, with real examples. The animation shows the parent nucleus (protons red, neutrons grey) emitting the radiation and becoming the daughter nucleus; the equation is written both in words and in nuclear notation, `\ _Z^A X`, where `A` (top) is the mass number (the number of nucleons) and `Z` (bottom) the atomic number (the number of protons, which sets the element).

In every decay both A and the charge are conserved: the top numbers and the bottom numbers each add up to the same total on both sides.

alpha: `\ _Z^A X -> \ _(Z-2)^(A-4) Y + \ _2^4 He`    beta-minus: `\ _Z^A X -> \ _(Z+1)^A Y + \ _(-1)^0 e + bar nu`

beta-plus: `\ _Z^A X -> \ _(Z-1)^A Y + \ _(+1)^0 e + nu`    gamma: `\ _Z^A X^** -> \ _Z^A X + gamma`

The “Predict first” button hides the equation until you have typed the daughter’s A and Z. The small chart shows where each decay moves the nucleus: alpha 2 down and 2 left, beta-minus up and left, beta-plus down and right, gamma nowhere. Energies (Q values) and half-lives are rounded.
