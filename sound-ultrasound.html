Ultrasound Imaging — Echoes, Scans and Doppler

Ultrasound is sound above 20 kHz; medical scanners use about 2 to 15 MHz. A transducer (a piezoelectric crystal) sends a short pulse into the body and then listens. At every boundary between tissues of different acoustic impedance Z = ρv, part of the pulse is reflected. Timing each echo gives the depth: d = vt/2, with v ≈ 1540 m/s in soft tissue. No ionizing radiation is involved.

Safety

Ultrasound uses no ionizing radiation and has no known harmful effects at diagnostic levels. The energy can still warm tissue a little (thermal index) and, at high pressure, make tiny gas bubbles oscillate (mechanical index); both are shown on the scanner screen.

Doppler uses more energy than plain imaging, so in early pregnancy it is used briefly. The rule is ALARA: As Low As Reasonably Achievable, and only for a medical reason (keepsake 3D videos are discouraged).

Medical ultrasound uses sound of 2 to 15 MHz. A transducer sends short pulses and records the echoes. At a boundary between materials of acoustic impedances `Z_1` and `Z_2` (with `Z = rho v`), the fraction of the intensity reflected is `R = ((Z_2 - Z_1)/(Z_2 + Z_1))^2`: tiny between soft tissues, large at bone, and almost total at air, which is why gel is used and why lungs and bowel gas block the view. The depth of a reflector follows from the echo time, `d = v t/2`, with `v ~~ 1540` m/s.

Higher frequencies (shorter wavelengths, `lambda = v/f`) give finer detail but are absorbed more (about 0.5 dB per cm per MHz), so they do not penetrate as deep. Sweeping the beam builds a B-mode image in real time. Fluids are black, soft tissues grey and speckled, bone, stones and air bright with an acoustic shadow behind. Enhancements include harmonic imaging, spatial compounding, contrast microbubbles, M-mode for moving structures, and 3D/4D surface rendering.

Moving blood shifts the frequency of the echoes by `Delta f = 2 f_0 v cos theta / c`. Colour Doppler paints flow toward the probe red and away blue; spectral Doppler shows the velocities against time and can be heard. The angle `theta` must be known and kept below about 60°, and velocities above the Nyquist limit alias. Ultrasound uses no ionizing radiation; doses are kept As Low As Reasonably Achievable.
