Readout

A delivery robot leaves its depot in the south of an old town and must bring a parcel to an address. Addresses are given as position vectors from the marker in front of City Hall, in the middle of the esplanade: `vec A = (x, y)`, in metres, with `x` toward the east and `y` toward the north. The depot's own position vector `vec D` is given the same way.

The robot moves only in straight lines, and only along the streets. Its trip planner takes a list of displacement vectors, one per leg, `vec d_1, vec d_2, ...`, and the robot follows them head to tail. It ends up at

`vec D + vec d_1 + vec d_2 + ... + vec d_n`,

so the legs must add up to the displacement from the depot to the address: `vec d_1 + vec d_2 + ... + vec d_n = vec A - vec D`. Each leg must also stay on the streets: a leg that would cut across buildings, the river or the town wall stops the robot where it would leave the street.

Tap an intersection to see its position vector; the displacement for a leg from one intersection to the next is the difference of their position vectors. The old streets meet at all sorts of angles, so the components of the legs are rarely simple; the coordinate grid can help. Once the parcel is delivered, the plan can be sent to the answer box: one vector per line, written x,y.

Answer box (demonstration). In the MOM version this is the question’s answer box [AB0]; Send the plan to the answer box fills it with the vectors, one per line (x,y). In MOM, the town and the address can be chosen with the variables $initIdx (the town) and $addrIdx (the address), or the address set directly with $tx and $ty.
