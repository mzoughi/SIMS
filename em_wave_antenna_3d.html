The propagation direction k̂ is no longer fixed — it is always kept perpendicular to the antenna (the direction of maximum, broadside radiation), and is recomputed automatically whenever the antenna's orientation changes. ψ lets you spin k̂ around the antenna axis to choose which broadside direction to look along; a dipole radiates equally in every direction around its own axis, so this does not change the amplitude. Animation speed is illustrative, not real time; B is drawn to the same visual length as E for clarity (true B₀=E₀/c is far smaller).

An antenna, driven by an oscillating current `I_0`, sends out an electromagnetic wave. Here the direction of propagation `hat k` follows the antenna: it is always kept perpendicular to the antenna (broadside, where a dipole radiates most), and is recomputed when the antenna's tilt `theta` or azimuth `phi` changes. The spin `psi` turns `hat k` around the antenna's axis; a dipole radiates equally all around its axis, so this does not change the amplitude.

`vec E _|_ vec B _|_ hat k`,   `c = lambda f`,   `B_0 = E_0/c`.
