A tensegrity structure is made of rigid struts that do not touch each other, held in place by a network of cables. The struts are pushed on (compression); the cables pull (tension). The structure stands only because every joint is in equilibrium: the forces of the members that meet there, together with any weight or load at that joint, add up to zero,

`sum vec F = 0` at every joint.

A cable behaves like a very stiff spring that can only pull. If it is stretched beyond its natural (rest) length `L_0`, its tension is

`T = k (L - L_0)`,   `k = (EA)/L_0`,

and if it is shorter than `L_0` it goes slack, `T = 0`: a cable cannot push. The cables are made slightly shorter than the gaps they span (the pretension), so all of them are stretched and pull, even with no load. A strut is much stiffer and can both push and pull.

For the structure as a whole, the ground pushes up on the joints that touch it. These normal forces add up to the total weight, `sum N = W_(structure) + W_(load)`; the readout checks this.

The designs. The 3-strut and 4-strut prisms are the simplest tensegrities: a ring of cables at the bottom, one at the top, and side cables, with the struts leaning between them. The 6-strut icosahedron has three pairs of parallel struts and 24 cables. The floating table is two rigid pieces that never touch: the top piece hangs from a single central cable, and four corner cables keep it from tipping. For the top piece, the central cable must hold up its weight, the load, and the downward pull of the four corner cables: `T_(centre) = W_(top) + W_(load) + T_1 + T_2 + T_3 + T_4`.

Failure. A cable snaps when its tension exceeds its breaking strength. A strut buckles (bends and gives way) when its compression exceeds its buckling limit. Either way the remaining members take on new forces, and they may fail in turn or the structure may collapse. Moving the load off centre makes it exert a torque: cables on one side carry more tension, those on the other side less, and a cable may go slack. Cut a single cable to see that every member is needed.

The numbers: struts 1 m long, 0.5 kg each; the table pieces 2 kg each. The SIM finds the equilibrium shape step by step; the motion shown while it settles or collapses is slowed down and is not real-time motion.
