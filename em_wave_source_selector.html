A light source, linearly, circularly or elliptically polarized, or unpolarized (eight random, mutually incoherent sub-waves), sends a wave that passes through up to three optical elements placed along its path: linear polarizers, half-wave and quarter-wave plates, general waveplates (linear retarders) and Faraday rotators. Each element's table row shows it face on, with its own axis in grey and, in red, the shape of the light's polarization just after it: a line for linear polarization, an ellipse or circle for elliptical or circular polarization, a dot when the light is blocked.

Unpolarized light loses half its intensity through a first polarizer. A polarizer passes only the component of `vec E` along its axis, so the intensity follows Malus's law; a waveplate delays one component relative to the other by its retardance `delta` (`pi` for a half-wave plate, `pi/2` for a quarter-wave plate); a Faraday rotator turns the polarization by its angle.
