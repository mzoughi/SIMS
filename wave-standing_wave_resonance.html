A hand shakes one end of a rope up and down at a frequency `f` that you choose. Waves travel along the rope at the speed `v = sqrt(T // mu)`, set by the tension `T` and the mass per unit length `mu`, reflect at the far end, and come back. The wave going out and the reflected wave add up.

At most frequencies they do not fit: the rope moves irregularly with a small amplitude. At particular frequencies, the resonant frequencies, the reflected waves arrive in step with the hand, and a standing wave builds up: some points do not move at all (nodes), others oscillate with a large amplitude (antinodes).

With both ends fixed, a whole number of half wavelengths must fit on the rope: `L = n lambda // 2`, so `f_n = n v // (2L)`, `n = 1, 2, 3, ...`. With a free far end (a ring sliding on a pole) the far end is an antinode, and an odd number of quarter wavelengths must fit: `f_n = (2n - 1) v // (4L)`.

Tune the frequency slowly to find each resonance; count the loops; use the rulers to measure the wavelength; and see how the tension and the mass per unit length change the resonant frequencies.
