Readout

Far from any planet, no force acts on a spacecraft unless it fires its thrusters. By Newton’s first law, it then keeps moving in a straight line at a constant velocity: it needs no force to keep going. The motion diagram shows this as images equally spaced in time and in distance.

While a thruster fires, the force changes the velocity: by Newton’s second law, `vec a = vec F / m`. The same force gives a heavier craft a smaller acceleration. When the force stops, the acceleration stops at once, but the velocity keeps the value it has reached. On the graphs, the velocity changes only where the force graph is not zero, and the change in velocity equals the area under the force graph divided by the mass: `Delta v = (F Delta t) / m`. A short, strong push (a kick) and a longer push can change the velocity by the same amount.

Your mission: plan your thrusters so that your craft (red) moves exactly like the target (cyan). Watch how the target moves, work out the force it must have felt and when, then match it.
