How to use: drag any charge, the yellow sensor cross, or the lime test charge. Click a charge once to select it (then adjust its magnitude above); double-click, or use the arrow keys plus Delete, to remove it. Use the tabs to switch between point charges, a parallel-plate capacitor, and concentric charged rings — you can still add extra point charges in either configuration.

Electric charges surround themselves with an electric potential `V` and an electric field `vec E`. The coloured map shows the potential (warm where it is positive, cool where it is negative); the field lines show the direction of the field, from positive charges toward negative ones. A sensor reads `V`, `|vec E|` and its components wherever it is placed, and a test charge `q` shows the force on it.

An equipotential is a line along which `V` is the same everywhere. The field is always perpendicular to the equipotentials and points toward lower potential, strongest where the equipotentials are closest together:

`vec F = q vec E`,   `E ~~ - (Delta V)/(Delta s)`.

Three set-ups are available: point charges you place and drag, a parallel-plate capacitor (between long plates the field is nearly uniform, as for infinite sheets with `|E| = |sigma_1 - sigma_2|` in the sim's units, `sigma = Q/L`), and concentric charged rings. Trace a few equipotentials with the sensor before the field lines unlock.
