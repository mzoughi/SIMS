What's happening

A solid sphere (mass 100 g, radius 15 cm) sits at the top of a 30° ramp. Its moment of inertia is I = 2/5 mr². Press Roll to release it. All gravitational potential energy converts to translational and rotational kinetic energy as it rolls without slipping to the bottom.

Rolling without slipping: v = ωr  |  KEtotal = ½mv² + ½Iω²  |  v = √(2gh / (1 + I/mr²))

This simulation shows an object rolling without slipping down an inclined ramp. Its shape (solid sphere, solid cylinder, thin hoop or spherical shell), mass, radius and the angle of the ramp can be changed.

A rolling object has kinetic energy of translation and of rotation. With no slipping, `v = omega r`, and with a moment of inertia `I = c m r^2`, energy conservation gives

`m g h = 1/2 m v^2 + 1/2 I omega^2 = 1/2 (1 + c) m v^2`,   so  `v = sqrt((2 g h)/(1 + c))`.

The factor `c` is `2/5` for a solid sphere, `1/2` for a solid cylinder, `2/3` for a spherical shell and `1` for a thin hoop. The speed at the bottom does not depend on the mass or the radius, only on the shape: the solid sphere wins and the hoop comes last, because more of its energy goes into turning.

This simulation is useful for studying rolling motion, moment of inertia and conservation of energy.
