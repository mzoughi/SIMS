Sound is a longitudinal wave: the particles of the medium move back and forth along the direction the wave travels, forming compressions and rarefactions. The particles do not travel with the wave; only the pattern does.

The speed of sound depends on the medium and, in a gas, on its temperature. There is no sound in a vacuum. For a gas,

`v = sqrt((gamma R T)/M)`,   `v = f lambda`,

where `gamma` is the gas's ratio of heat capacities, `R` the gas constant, `T` the temperature and `M` the molar mass. Liquids and solids, which are much stiffer, carry sound faster. The wavelength guide, the tracked-particle markers, the compression and rarefaction points and the horizontal caliper help measure the wave; the ⏱ button times it.
