How it works
The numbers

Sound is a mechanical wave of pressure that needs a medium. Its speed depends on the medium (about 343 m/s in air at 20 °C, 1480 m/s in water, 5960 m/s in steel), and its frequency `f`, wavelength `lambda` and speed `v` are related by `v = f lambda`; the period is `T = 1/f`.

By frequency, sound is divided into infrasound (below 20 Hz), audible sound (20 Hz to 20 kHz for a young person), ultrasound (above 20 kHz) and hypersound (above about 1 GHz). Hearing ranges differ widely between species: elephants and whales use infrasound, bats, dolphins and some moths ultrasound. Human sensitivity is greatest near 3 to 4 kHz and the upper limit falls with age.

The applications tab shows uses across the spectrum: echolocation and sonar, parking sensors and flaw detection (distance from echo time, `d = vt/2`), ultrasonic cleaning by cavitation, focused ultrasound therapy, infrasound monitoring, musical harmonics and noise cancelling, and age-related hearing loss. Hearing ranges and source frequencies are approximate and vary between sources and individuals. The tone generator plays only audible frequencies; start with a low volume.
