This tool measures positions on several photographs or scanned graphs, each with its own scale: choose an image with the buttons above it (or add your own), set its scale by clicking two points of known value, then click to record positions (x only, y only, or both). Each image keeps its own scale, units and points; the table and the spacing are for the chosen image, and a summary table compares all the images.

The scale converts pixels to real units. For two reference points at pixels `p_1` and `p_2` with known values `X_1` and `X_2`,

`X = X_1 + (p - p_1)(X_2 - X_1)/(p_2 - p_1)`.

Because each image has its own reference points, images taken at different magnifications or distances can be compared directly. The spacing tool finds the average distance between equally spaced features, such as interference fringes or tick marks, with its uncertainty. Zoom and the magnifier help place each click precisely.
