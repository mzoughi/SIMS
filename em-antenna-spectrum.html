An antenna, driven by an oscillating current, sends out an electromagnetic wave. The simulation shows, in 3D, the electric field `vec E` along the direction of propagation `hat k` and, if chosen, the magnetic field `vec B`. The fields are perpendicular to each other and to `hat k`, and they oscillate in step. The antenna's angle `theta` sets the direction in which `vec E` oscillates: the wave's linear polarization.

The band selector runs through the electromagnetic spectrum, from radio waves to gamma rays, each with its realistic range of wavelengths. Every band travels at the same speed in vacuum, so the frequency follows from the wavelength:

`c = lambda f`,   `B_0 = E_0/c`.

In the visible band the wave takes the colour of its wavelength. The drawing always shows about three wavelengths, because real wavelengths span more than twelve orders of magnitude; and `vec B` is drawn as tall as `vec E` for clarity, though in SI units it is far smaller. Drag to turn the view, and scroll or pinch to zoom; the spectrum strip shows where the chosen wavelength sits.
