Why does the far end usually need to be closed to get a clean tone?

A closed end reflects almost everything; an open end leaks. At a rigid closed end the air has nowhere to go, so the pressure wave bounces back almost completely — a strong, low-loss resonance that lets a gentle breath build up into a loud, stable tone. An open end still reflects some of the wave (that mismatch between "confined in a tube" and "free in open air" is exactly why sound escapes there at all), but some energy is lost every round trip. Lose it at one end (closed–open) and the resonance stays strong; lose it at both ends (open–open) it's leakier, and harder to drive with something as gentle as a breath.

The bigger issue for a plain straw: there's no edge to catch the air. Blowing across an opening to get a tone (like blowing over a bottle) relies on an edge tone — the air jet has to hit a sharp edge and split into oscillating vortices that lock onto the tube's resonant frequency. A flute's embouchure hole and a bottle's rim are shaped to make that happen reliably. A plain drinking straw has no such edge: if the far end is open, most of the air you blow just travels straight down the bore and out the other side, like blowing through a pipe, instead of skimming an edge and building the backpressure needed to drive an oscillation. Capping the far end forces that air to stop and reflect, which is what reliably kicks the jet oscillation into a locked, audible resonance. It's a mechanical/practical reliability issue, not a "physics forbids it" one — open pipes genuinely do resonate, that's literally how a flute works — but getting a clean tone from an unshaped opening on something as narrow as a straw is finicky at best without a cap.

Why the reed sims (straw length, flute holes, trombone) don't have this problem: a reed is a self-driving oscillator — it flaps open and shut on its own regardless of how well the far end reflects. It doesn't need the delicate edge-tone coupling that blowing across an opening relies on, so it works fine with the far end open, which is exactly those sims' setup.

A caveat about this simulation: the "Open" setting here always plays a clean idealized tone at f = v/(2L), computed directly rather than modeling breath, jets, and edge turbulence. It doesn't capture how hard that setting actually is to excite in practice — on a real straw, blowing across an open far end would likely produce mostly breath noise rather than a clear note, for the reasons above. The physics number is correct; the simulation just doesn't model how difficult it is to reach in real life.
