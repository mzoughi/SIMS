Wave on a String: Crossing a Boundary Between Two Media

Frequency:
`f:\ ` 400 Hz
Amplitude:
`A:\ ` 20 cm
Tension:
`T:\ ` 100 N
Mass/length, rope 1:
`mu_1:\ ` 10 g/m
Mass/length, rope 2:
`mu_2:\ ` 30 g/m
  1. As the wave crosses from rope 1 into rope 2, does the wavelength get longer or shorter when `mu_2 > mu_1`? Why?
  2. What determines the wave speed on each rope? Turn on the horizontal caliper and measure `lambda_1` and `lambda_2`; check that `v=f lambda` holds on each side using the wave-data panel.
  3. How does the transmitted amplitude compare to the incident amplitude `A` when `mu_2 > mu_1`? What about when `mu_2 < mu_1`?
  4. The quantity `Z=mu v` is called the rope's impedance. Based on what you observe, how does the mismatch between `Z_1` and `Z_2` affect how much amplitude gets through?
  5. Set `mu_1` and `mu_2` to the same value. What happens to the wave as it crosses the (now invisible) boundary? Why does this make sense?
  6. Does the frequency change as the wave crosses into rope 2? Explain why frequency must stay the same even though wavelength changes.
  7. Turn on the tracked-point markers and let the animation run. Do the marked points travel horizontally with the wave, or only move vertically?
  8. How does increasing the tension `T` affect `lambda_1` and `lambda_2` together? Why does a tension change affect both ropes the same way?

This simulation sends a continuous wave along a rope made of two parts joined together, rope 1 and rope 2, with different masses per unit length. The frequency, the amplitude, the tension and the masses per unit length can be changed.

The speed of the wave in each rope is

`v = sqrt(T/mu)`,

When the wave crosses into the second rope its speed changes, but its frequency does not: every point of the joint moves up and down at the frequency of the source. Since

`v = f lambda`,

the wavelength changes in proportion to the speed: the wave gets shorter in the slower (heavier) rope. Part of the wave is also reflected at the joint.

The wavelength guide, the calipers and the timer help measure the wavelength, the period and the speed in each rope.
