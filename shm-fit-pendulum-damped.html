This simulation measures a damped oscillation the way an experiment would: each data point carries a little random noise, and the true parameters are hidden. The motion follows

`theta(t) = A e^(-beta t) cos(2 pi f t + phi)`,

with an amplitude `A`, a frequency `f` (the period is `T = 1/f`), a phase `phi` and a damping coefficient `beta`. After collecting the data, adjust the four sliders of the fit curve until it follows the data as closely as possible; the RMS (root-mean-square) residual measures how far the data points are from the curve, and is smallest for the best fit. Good starting points: the time between successive peaks gives `T`, the first peak gives `A`, and the ratio of successive peaks, `e^(-beta T)`, gives `beta`.
