This simulation launches two pulses, A and B, from the two ends of one rope, so that they meet and pass through each other. Each pulse can be sent upward or downward, with its own amplitude; the pulses also reflect from the ends and meet again.

Where the pulses overlap, the displacement of the rope is the sum of their displacements (the principle of superposition):

`y = y_A + y_B`.

Two upward pulses add up to a taller pulse while they overlap (constructive interference); an upward and a downward pulse of the same size cancel for an instant, leaving the rope flat (destructive interference). After they meet, each pulse continues unchanged, as if the other had never been there.
