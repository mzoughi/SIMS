In this simulation, you can explore the electric potential due to point charges. The simulation shows three charges. A positive charge is indicated with a plus sign (+), if its corresponding slider is set to a positive value, and a negative charge with a minus sign (−), if its slider is set to a negative value. You can drag the charges around the screen to change their positions, and change the charges with the sliders.

A point charge `q` produces at a distance `r` the electric potential

`V = k q/r`,

positive near a positive charge and negative near a negative one. Potential is a scalar, so the potential of several charges is simply the sum, with signs:

`V = k (q_1/r_1 + q_2/r_2 + q_3/r_3)`.

Lines of equal potential (equipotentials) are always perpendicular to the electric field, which points from high potential toward low potential.
