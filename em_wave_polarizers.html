Position is distance along the propagation axis (k̂) measured from the antenna. Grey line(s) on each disc show the element's own axis (transmission axis for a polarizer, fast/slow axes for a waveplate). The red trace shows the actual shape of the light's polarization immediately after that element — a straight line for linear polarization, an open ellipse or circle for elliptical/circular polarization, and a vanishing dot when the light is fully blocked.

The linearly polarized wave from an antenna passes through up to three optical elements placed along its path: linear polarizers, half-wave and quarter-wave plates, general waveplates (linear retarders) and Faraday rotators. Each element's table row shows it face on, with its own axis in grey and, in red, the shape of the light's polarization just after it: a line for linear polarization, an ellipse or circle for elliptical or circular polarization, a dot when the light is blocked.

A polarizer passes only the component of `vec E` along its axis, so the intensity follows Malus's law; a waveplate delays one component relative to the other by its retardance `delta` (`pi` for a half-wave plate, `pi/2` for a quarter-wave plate); a Faraday rotator turns the polarization by its angle.

`I = I_0 cos^2 theta`.
